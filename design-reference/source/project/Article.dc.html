Abstract

Open to everyone

Sleep plays a central role in consolidating new memories, yet its effect on classroom learning in adolescents remains under-studied. We followed secondary-school students across a full academic term, pairing wearable sleep tracking with weekly retention tests. Students with more stable slow-wave sleep retained significantly more material at four-week follow-up, independent of study time. We discuss implications for school start times and homework scheduling.
